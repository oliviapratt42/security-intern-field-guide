Working vocabulary

Glossary

Because half of cybersecurity is knowing what the acronym means.

Concept

Access control

Rules and mechanisms that decide who or what can use a resource and what actions they can perform.

Threat

Advanced persistent threat (APT)

A well-resourced threat actor or campaign that maintains long-term access or targeting. The term is often used for nation-state or highly organized activity.

Concept

Asset

Something an organization values and needs to protect, such as a system, application, identity, database, or dataset.

Concept

Attack surface

The set of places where an attacker could interact with an organization, including services, APIs, users, vendors, dependencies, identities, and cloud resources.

Concept

Authentication

The process of proving identity with something like a password, multifactor prompt, certificate, or token.

Concept

Authorization

The process of deciding what an authenticated identity is allowed to do.

Concept

CIA triad

Confidentiality, integrity, and availability: a simple model for what security tries to protect.

Control

Control

A safeguard or process intended to reduce risk. Controls can be technical, administrative, or physical.

Operations

Endpoint

A user or server system, such as a laptop, desktop, virtual machine, or workload.

Operations

False negative

A missed alert or finding: harmful activity happened, but the control did not catch it.

Operations

False positive

An alert or finding that appears suspicious but is not actually harmful or relevant.

GRC

Governance

The way an organization defines security responsibilities, decision rights, policies, and oversight.

ConceptIAM

Identity and access management (IAM)

The systems and processes used to manage users, service accounts, roles, permissions, authentication, and authorization.

Operations

Incident

A security event that may harm confidentiality, integrity, availability, policy, compliance, or business operations.

OperationsProcess

Incident response (IR)

The organized process for handling a security incident, including triage, containment, investigation, communication, recovery, and lessons learned.

Threat

Indicator of compromise (IOC)

An artifact that may suggest malicious activity, such as a file hash, IP address, domain, URL, or suspicious process name.

Concept

Least privilege

The design principle that users, services, and systems should have only the access they need.

ControlIAM

Multifactor authentication (MFA)

Authentication that uses more than one kind of proof, such as something you know, have, or are.

Assessment

Penetration test (pentest)

An authorized security assessment that attempts to validate exploitable weaknesses in a defined scope. A good pentest produces evidence, impact, and practical remediation guidance.

AssessmentProcess

Rules of engagement (RoE)

The written boundaries for a test or exercise: what is in scope, what is off limits, when testing may happen, who to contact, how far testers may go, and how to handle sensitive findings.

Control

Patch management

The process of identifying, testing, prioritizing, and applying updates that fix bugs or vulnerabilities.

GRC

Risk

The possibility that a threat could exploit a weakness and cause impact, usually discussed through likelihood, impact, and context.

Team

Red team

A security team or exercise role that uses authorized attacker-informed methods to test assumptions, validate risk, and improve defenses.

Team

Blue team

A defensive security team or exercise role focused on monitoring, detection, response, hardening, and protecting systems.

Team

Purple team

A collaborative approach where offensive and defensive teams work together to improve detection, response, and control effectiveness.

Ethics

White hat hacker

A hacker who works with authorization and ethical intent, usually to find and report weaknesses so they can be fixed.

Ethics

Black hat hacker

A hacker who acts without authorization and with harmful, criminal, or exploitative intent.

Ethics

Grey hat hacker

A hacker whose actions fall between clear authorization and clear malicious intent, often creating legal, ethical, or safety concerns.

Role

Chief information security officer (CISO)

The senior leader responsible for an organization's security strategy, risk posture, program priorities, and executive-level security communication.

Role

Security analyst

A role focused on reviewing security information, investigating activity, documenting findings, and helping teams decide what needs attention.

Role

Security architect

A senior security role focused on designing secure systems and programs. Architects help choose patterns, controls, tradeoffs, and standards before systems are built or changed.

Role

Security engineer

A role focused on building, configuring, automating, and maintaining security systems and controls so they work reliably at organizational scale.

TeamOperations

Security operations center (SOC)

A team or function that monitors security activity, investigates alerts, escalates incidents, and coordinates response work.

Team

Security operations (SecOps)

The day-to-day work of monitoring, investigating, triaging, escalating, and responding to security activity across an organization.

Operations

Security information and event management (SIEM)

A system that collects and analyzes security-relevant events so teams can investigate activity, create alerts, and report on patterns.

ConceptAppSec

Shifting left

Moving security work earlier in the development process so teams can catch design, code, dependency, and configuration issues before release.

TeamAppSec

DevSecOps

An approach that integrates security into development and operations workflows through automation, shared responsibility, and secure delivery practices.

Operations

Telemetry

Observable data from systems and tools, such as events, metrics, traces, alerts, and network records.

Threat

Threat actor

An individual or group that could cause harm to systems or data.

Threat

Threat intelligence (TI)

Contextual information about threats that helps an organization make decisions.

Concept

Vulnerability

A weakness that could be exploited to harm confidentiality, integrity, availability, or trust.

Program

Vulnerability management (VM)

The process of discovering, prioritizing, fixing, tracking, and reporting vulnerabilities across an organization.
