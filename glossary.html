Glossary

This glossary gives beginner-friendly working definitions. It is not a certification cram sheet. Use it to recognize vocabulary when reading branch pages, job descriptions, public reports, and security resources.

Access control

Rules and mechanisms that decide who or what can access a resource and what actions they can perform.

Asset

Something an organization values and needs to protect, such as a server, laptop, application, identity, database, cloud account, or dataset.

Attack surface

The set of places where an attacker could interact with a system, such as exposed services, login pages, APIs, dependencies, users, or cloud resources.

Authentication

The process of proving identity. Passwords, multifactor prompts, certificates, and tokens can all be part of authentication.

Authorization

The process of deciding what an authenticated identity is allowed to do. A user may be logged in but still not allowed to access a specific record or admin function.

Availability

The security objective that systems and data should be accessible when needed.

CIA triad

Confidentiality, integrity, and availability. A simple model for thinking about what security is trying to protect.

Control

A safeguard or process intended to reduce risk. Controls can be technical, administrative, or physical.

Detection

The practice of noticing suspicious or harmful activity by using logs, alerts, behavioral patterns, and investigation.

Encryption

A reversible way to protect data so that only someone with the right key can read it.

Encoding

A way to represent data in a different format. Encoding is not meant to provide secrecy.

Endpoint

A user or server system such as a laptop, desktop, virtual machine, or workload where activity can be monitored or protected.

Evidence

Information that supports a security conclusion. In security work, evidence may include logs, screenshots, packet captures, alerts, tickets, configuration exports, or command output.

False negative

A missed detection. Something bad happened, but the control or alert did not catch it.

False positive

An alert or finding that appears suspicious but is not actually harmful or relevant.

Governance

The way an organization defines security responsibilities, decision rights, policies, and oversight.

Hashing

A one-way process that turns data into a fixed-length value. Hashing is commonly used for integrity checks and password storage, but it is not encryption.

Identity and access management

The systems and processes used to manage users, service accounts, roles, permissions, authentication, and authorization.

Incident

A security event that may harm confidentiality, integrity, availability, policy, compliance, or business operations.

Indicator of compromise

An artifact that may suggest malicious activity, such as a file hash, IP address, domain, URL, registry key, or suspicious process name.

Least privilege

The design principle that users, services, and systems should have only the access they need to do their job.

Log

A record of events from a system, application, network device, cloud service, identity provider, or security tool.

Malware

Software designed to harm, spy on, disrupt, steal, extort, or give unauthorized control.

Multifactor authentication

Authentication that uses more than one kind of proof, such as something you know, something you have, or something you are.

Patch management

The process of identifying, testing, prioritizing, and applying software updates that fix bugs or vulnerabilities.

Risk

The possibility that a threat could exploit a weakness and cause impact. Risk is usually discussed in terms of likelihood, impact, and context.

Security information and event management

A SIEM collects and analyzes security-relevant logs so teams can investigate and alert on activity across systems.

Telemetry

Observable data from systems and tools, such as logs, events, metrics, traces, alerts, and network records.

Threat actor

An individual or group that could cause harm to systems or data. Threat actors may be criminals, insiders, nation-state groups, hacktivists, or opportunistic attackers.

Threat intelligence

Contextual information about threats that helps an organization make decisions.

Vulnerability

A weakness that could be exploited to harm confidentiality, integrity, availability, or trust.

Vulnerability management

The process of discovering, prioritizing, fixing, tracking, and reporting vulnerabilities across an organization.
